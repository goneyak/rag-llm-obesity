# Obesity Management Guidelines
# Source: Adapted from WHO, CDC, and AHA public health recommendations
# For educational and research purposes

## 1. Definition and Classification of Obesity

Obesity is defined as abnormal or excessive fat accumulation that presents a health risk.
Body Mass Index (BMI) is commonly used for population-level classification:
- BMI < 18.5: Underweight
- BMI 18.5–24.9: Normal weight
- BMI 25.0–29.9: Overweight (Pre-obese)
- BMI 30.0–34.9: Obesity Class I
- BMI 35.0–39.9: Obesity Class II (Severe obesity)
- BMI ≥ 40: Obesity Class III (Morbid obesity)

Waist circumference is an additional indicator of abdominal obesity risk:
- Men: > 102 cm (40 inches) indicates high risk
- Women: > 88 cm (35 inches) indicates high risk

## 2. Causes and Risk Factors

Obesity is a multifactorial disease involving:

**Behavioral factors:**
- High caloric intake (especially ultra-processed foods high in fat and sugar)
- Low physical activity (sedentary lifestyle, excessive screen time)
- Irregular meal patterns and frequent snacking between meals
- Alcohol consumption contributing excess calories
- Poor sleep quality or insufficient sleep duration (< 7 hours per night)

**Genetic and family history:**
- Family history of overweight or obesity significantly increases individual risk
- Genetic predisposition affects fat distribution and metabolic rate

**Environmental and socioeconomic factors:**
- Limited access to healthy foods (food deserts)
- Built environments that discourage physical activity
- High stress and poor mental health (emotional eating)

**Medical factors:**
- Hypothyroidism, Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS), Cushing's syndrome
- Medications: corticosteroids, antidepressants, antipsychotics, insulin
- Gut microbiome composition

## 3. Health Consequences of Obesity

Obesity is associated with numerous serious conditions:

**Metabolic:**
- Type 2 diabetes mellitus
- Insulin resistance and metabolic syndrome
- Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD)
- Dyslipidemia (high LDL, low HDL, high triglycerides)

**Cardiovascular:**
- Hypertension (high blood pressure)
- Coronary artery disease and heart failure
- Stroke and peripheral artery disease

**Musculoskeletal:**
- Osteoarthritis of the knee and hip
- Back pain and disc herniation

**Respiratory:**
- Obstructive sleep apnea
- Obesity hypoventilation syndrome

**Psychological:**
- Depression and anxiety
- Reduced quality of life and social stigma

**Cancer:**
- Increased risk of colorectal, breast, endometrial, kidney, and esophageal cancers

## 4. Dietary Interventions

**General dietary principles for weight management:**
- Create a sustained caloric deficit of 500–750 kcal/day to achieve ~0.5–0.7 kg/week weight loss
- Prioritize whole foods: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, lean proteins, legumes
- Limit added sugars to < 25g/day (WHO recommendation)
- Reduce saturated and trans fats; increase unsaturated fats (olive oil, avocado, nuts)
- Limit sodium intake to < 2,300 mg/day to manage blood pressure
- Stay well hydrated: drink at least 2–2.5 liters of water per day
- Eat slowly and mindfully; avoid distractions during meals
- Avoid calorie-dense, nutrient-poor foods: fast food, sugary beverages, chips

**Evidence-based dietary patterns:**
- Mediterranean diet: rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, fish, olive oil
- DASH (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension): emphasizes fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy
- Low-glycemic index diet: stabilizes blood sugar and reduces hunger
- High-protein diets (25–30% of calories): increase satiety, preserve muscle mass during caloric deficit

**Portion control strategies:**
- Use smaller plates to reduce serving sizes
- Fill half the plate with non-starchy vegetables
- Keep food diary to track intakes
- Avoid food rewards and eating out of boredom

## 5. Physical Activity Recommendations

**For general health maintenance:**
- At least 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week
- OR 75–150 minutes of vigorous-intensity aerobic activity per week
- Muscle-strengthening activities (resistance training) ≥ 2 days per week

**For weight loss and maintenance:**
- 300+ minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity recommended for significant weight loss
- Combine aerobic exercise with resistance training for optimal body composition

**Reducing sedentary behavior:**
- Break up sitting time every 30–60 minutes with light activity
- Limit screen time (TV, smartphones) to less than 3 hours per day
- Incorporate incidental activity: take stairs, walk during phone calls, cycle to work

**Exercise prescription by obesity class:**
- BMI 25–30: 30–45 min moderate aerobic daily, 2x strength training/week
- BMI 30–35: start with low-impact activities (walking, swimming, cycling); 30 min daily
- BMI 35–40: water aerobics, seated exercises, chair yoga; progress gradually
- BMI ≥ 40: supervised exercise programs; focus on mobility and preventing injury first

## 6. Behavioral and Psychological Interventions

**Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT):**
- Identify and change maladaptive thoughts and behaviors related to food and activity
- Develop coping strategies for emotional and stress-related eating
- Set realistic, specific, and measurable goals

**Motivational Interviewing:**
- Build intrinsic motivation for lifestyle change
- Resolve ambivalence about weight management
- Strengthen commitment to behavioral goals

**Self-monitoring:**
- Regular weigh-ins (weekly preferred over daily to reduce anxiety)
- Food and activity journaling
- Use wearable devices and health apps for accountability

**Social support:**
- Involve family and friends in lifestyle changes
- Group-based programs show better long-term adherence than individual programs
- Online support communities can supplement in-person care

## 7. Medical Management

**Pharmacotherapy (BMI ≥ 30, or BMI ≥ 27 with comorbidity):**

*GLP-1 receptor agonists (first-line):*
- Semaglutide (Wegovy): up to 15% body weight reduction in trials; weekly injection
- Liraglutide (Saxenda): 5–10% weight loss; daily injection
- Tirzepatide (Zepbound): dual GIP/GLP-1 agonist; up to 20% weight reduction

*Other approved agents:*
- Naltrexone-bupropion (Contrave): reduces appetite and cravings
- Phentermine-topiramate (Qsymia): short-term appetite suppression
- Orlistat (Xenical): reduces fat absorption by ~30%

Pharmacotherapy is always adjunct to lifestyle modification, not a standalone treatment.

**Monitoring during weight loss treatment:**
- Blood glucose, HbA1c (especially if diabetic)
- Lipid panel every 3–6 months
- Blood pressure, heart rate
- Liver function tests (if fatty liver suspected)
- Kidney function if on certain medications

## 8. Bariatric Surgery

**Eligibility criteria:**
- BMI ≥ 40, or BMI ≥ 35 with obesity-related comorbidity (T2DM, sleep apnea, hypertension)
- Failure of conventional weight loss treatments over 6+ months
- Psychologically stable; no active substance abuse

**Common procedures:**
- Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass: gold standard; 60–80% excess weight loss; reduces hunger hormones
- Sleeve Gastrectomy: 50–60% excess weight loss; simpler procedure; no malabsorption
- Adjustable Gastric Band: 40–50% excess weight loss; fully reversible; slower results
- Biliopancreatic Diversion with Duodenal Switch: most effective (70–80%) but highest complication risk

**Post-surgical care:**
- Lifelong vitamin and mineral supplementation (B12, D, iron, calcium)
- Regular follow-up with multidisciplinary team
- Psychological support to address body image and eating behaviors

## 9. Childhood and Adolescent Obesity

**Key differences from adult management:**
- BMI-for-age percentiles used instead of absolute BMI
- Overweight: 85th–94th percentile; Obese: ≥ 95th percentile
- Focus on healthy growth rather than strict weight loss
- Family-based interventions are most effective (involve parents and caregivers)

**Recommendations:**
- Limit sugar-sweetened beverages; encourage water and low-fat milk
- Structured meal times; no screens during meals
- At least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity daily
- Limit recreational screen time to < 2 hours/day
- Ensure adequate sleep: 9–11 hours for school-age children, 8–10 hours for teens

## 10. Long-Term Weight Maintenance

Weight regain after loss is common. Evidence-based strategies for maintenance:
- Maintain lifestyle changes (diet and physical activity) indefinitely
- Self-monitor weight weekly; regain threshold: if +2–3 kg from goal, resume active intervention
- High physical activity (60+ min/day) is critical for maintenance—more than for initial weight loss
- Continue any effective pharmacotherapy long-term
- Regular follow-up with healthcare providers (every 3–6 months initially, then annually)
- Build resilient habits rather than relying on willpower; modify the food environment

## 11. Patient Education Key Messages

1. Obesity is a chronic disease, not a personal failure—seek professional support
2. Sustainable weight loss of 5–10% of body weight significantly reduces health risks
3. Small, consistent changes are more effective long-term than extreme diets
4. Regular physical activity is essential for both weight management and overall health
5. Mental health support is a critical component of obesity treatment
6. Sleep quality and stress management directly impact weight regulation
7. Avoid fad diets; choose evidence-based dietary patterns
8. Involve your healthcare team: physician, dietitian, psychologist, physiotherapist
